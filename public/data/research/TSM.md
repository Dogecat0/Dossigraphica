## 1. Geographic Profile Summary

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSMC) is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, headquartered in **Hsinchu, Taiwan**. The company commands a dominant global footprint, holding approximately **70% of the global foundry market share** and manufacturing **92% of all logic chips at 10nm or smaller nodes**.

In 2024, the geographic revenue split was: North America 70%, China 11%, Asia Pacific (ex-China/Japan) 10%, Japan 5%, and EMEA 4%.

### Global Manufacturing Footprint

TSMC operates advanced fabrication facilities across multiple continents:

- **Taiwan** – Primary manufacturing base with advanced fabs
- **Arizona, USA** – Expanding overseas production capacity
- **Kumamoto, Japan** – New fab operations
- **Dresden, Germany** – European expansion underway

The company produces **over 12,000 different products** for customers worldwide, with overseas expansion accelerating its global manufacturing presence and reducing geographic concentration risk.

### Concentration Risk

TSMC faces significant geographic concentration risk. The **United States** alone represents **74.4%** of total revenue, driven overwhelmingly by demand from major AI and smartphone customers. This dependency is amplified by customer concentration: **Nvidia** and **Apple** are estimated to account for approximately **22%** and **18%** of TSMC's revenue, respectively, making the company highly sensitive to the fortunes of its two largest clients.

**China** (8.6% of revenue) presents a distinct risk vector. Revenue from China declined 1.3% year-over-year, reflecting ongoing export controls and geopolitical tensions that restrict TSMC's ability to supply advanced chips to Chinese customers. Any further tightening of trade restrictions could further pressure this segment.

### 4.9 Key Insights

1. **Single points of failure:** ASML (EUV lithography), Japanese photoresist firms, and TSMC itself represent critical chokepoints where disruption would cascade globally.
2. **US equipment dominance:** The concentration of wafer fab equipment and EDA software in US firms provides significant geopolitical leverage.
3. **Taiwan's irreplaceable role:** No single country or region currently possesses the ecosystem depth to replicate Taiwan's semiconductor cluster.
4. **Geographic diversification underway:** TSMC's fabs in Arizona, Kumamoto, and Dresden represent a strategic hedge, but advanced node production remains overwhelmingly in Taiwan.

## 5. Customer Concentration

**Apple Inc.** (Cupertino, United States) has historically been TSMC's largest customer, contributing **22% of TSMC's net revenue in 2024**. Apple relies on TSMC as its primary foundry for A-series chips (iPhones) and M-series chips (Macs and servers), a relationship dating back to 2016.

**NVIDIA Corporation** (Santa Clara, United States) was TSMC's second-largest customer in 2024 at **12% of net revenue**, but is projected to become the largest customer in 2025, with its share rising to **19%** (surpassing Apple's projected 17% share in 2025). This shift reflects the explosive growth in demand for NVIDIA's AI and GPU chips manufactured by TSMC.

### Geographic Concentration

The customer base is heavily weighted toward **United States-based companies**, which dominate the top tier. Key US customers include Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm, Intel, Marvell, Texas Instruments, and Tesla. This geographic concentration underscores TSMC's strategic importance to the US semiconductor ecosystem.

**European customers** include NXP (Netherlands), STMicroelectronics (Switzerland), Infineon (Germany), and Bosch (Germany)—several of whom are joint venture partners in TSMC's European Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (ESMC) fab project in Dresden, Germany.

### Former Customer: HiSilicon

**HiSilicon Technologies Co., Ltd.** (Shenzhen, China), the chip design arm of Huawei, was a major TSMC customer until **September 2020**, when shipments were discontinued due to US export controls. HiSilicon previously relied on TSMC as its main foundry provider. This loss illustrates the geopolitical vulnerability embedded in TSMC's customer relationships.

### Concentration Risk

With Apple and NVIDIA alone representing over 30% of TSMC's revenue (and projected to exceed 35% in 2025), the company faces significant customer concentration risk. Any disruption in demand from either customer—due to market shifts, geopolitical events, or technology transitions—could materially impact TSMC's financial performance. However, the deep technological interdependence and TSMC's manufacturing leadership create high switching costs that anchor these relationships.

## 6. Regulatory Risk

TSMC operates at the intersection of intensifying US-China technology competition, Taiwanese sovereignty dynamics, and a rapidly evolving global export control regime. The company faces a complex web of regulatory obligations that span multiple jurisdictions, with critical implications for its customer base, supply chain, and strategic flexibility.

### 6.1 US Export Controls & Compliance

**US Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR) & BIS Licensing Regime**  
*Impact Level: Critical | Risk Score: 5/5*

TSMC must comply with the US Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR) due to the presence of US-origin technology in its semiconductor manufacturing tools. The Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) October 2022 and 2023 rules, reinforced by January 2025 regulations, require TSMC to obtain export licenses for chips using 16nm or below process nodes shipped to global destinations unless specific conditions are met. TSMC halted shipments to Huawei in September 2020, and a potential $1B+ penalty looms from the Sophgo/Huawei diversion incident.

> *Filing Reference:* [TSMC 2024 Annual Report, p. 147](https://investor.tsmc.com/static/annualReports/2024/english/ebook/files/basic-html/page147.html)

**US AI Diffusion Rule & Licensing Uncertainty**  
*Impact Level: Moderate | Risk Score: 3/5*

The January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule categorized countries into three tiers, with only 18 key allies facing no restrictions. TSMC faced potential licensing requirements for 16nm or below chips to any global destination, with rules applying to AI model "weights." Although BIS rescinded the rule in May 2025, the regulatory uncertainty persists and could resurface.

> *Filing Reference:* [Global Taiwan Institute, March 2025](https://globaltaiwan.org/2025/03/how-taiwans-chip-industry-navigates-us-industrial-policy-and-export-controls/)

**CHIPS Act Restrictions on China Expansion**  
*Impact Level: High | Risk Score: 4/5*

TSMC's receipt of subsidies under the US CHIPS Act prohibits the company from expanding manufacturing in China. The Act bars recipients from increasing production of chips more advanced than 28nm in China and Russia for 10 years. TSMC's Validated End-User (VEU) authorization for its Nanjing fab may be terminated in the future, further constraining its China strategy.

> *Filing Reference:* [Global Taiwan Institute, March 2025](https://globaltaiwan.org/2025/03/how-taiwans-chip-industry-navigates-us-industrial-policy-and-export-controls/)

### 6.2 Taiwan & China Regulatory Dynamics

**Taiwan's Export Control Entity List Expansion**  
*Impact Level: High | Risk Score: 4/5*

On June 10, 2025, Taiwan added 601 entities from Russia, Pakistan, Iran, Myanmar, and China to its Strategic High-Tech Commodities (SHTC) entity list, including Huawei and SMIC. TSMC must now secure government licenses before exporting to these entities. This alignment with US-led controls could expose Taiwan to WTO litigation from China.

> *Filing Reference:* [The Diplomat, July 2025](https://thediplomat.com/2025/07/silicon-statecraft-alignment-taiwans-strategic-bet-on-us-led-export-controls/)

**Chinese Retaliation & Blocking Statutes**  
*Impact Level: Moderate | Risk Score: 3/5*

China adopted a blocking statute in January 2021 that entitles Chinese entities to seek civil remedies from multinationals complying with foreign laws. China has opened antitrust investigations against Nvidia (December 2024) and Qualcomm (October 2025), and an antidumping investigation on US analog chips (September 2025). China also uses export controls on germanium, gallium, and rare earth magnets as retaliatory tools, directly impacting TSMC's supply chain.

> *Filing Reference:* [TSMC 2024 Annual Report, p. 147](https://investor.tsmc.com/static/annualReports/2024/english/ebook/files/basic-html/page147.html)

**Loss of Chinese Market & Customer Base**  
*Impact Level: High | Risk Score: 4/5*

US export controls restrict TSMC from selling advanced AI/HPC chips to Chinese customers such as Alibaba, Baidu, and Huawei — the latter was TSMC's second-largest customer before the 2020 ban. China's antitrust investigations and antidumping probes against US chip firms create ongoing retaliatory risk.

> *Filing Reference:* [TSMC 2024 Annual Report, p. 147](https://investor.tsmc.com/static/annualReports/2024/english/ebook/files/basic-html/page147.html)

### 6.3 Tariff & Trade Policy Risks

**US Tariffs on Taiwanese Semiconductors**  
*Impact Level: High | Risk Score: 4/5*

President Trump has threatened to place tariffs on all Taiwanese semiconductors and announced intentions for more expansive tariffs. Tariffs on semiconductor imports to the US would increase costs and decrease demand for TSMC's products. The "reciprocal tariffs" policy targeting countries with goods trade deficits creates significant uncertainty for TSMC's US-bound shipments.

> *Filing Reference:* [TSMC 2024 Annual Report, p. 147](https://investor.tsmc.com/static/annualReports/2024/english/ebook/files/basic-html/page147.html)

**Global Trade Fragmentation & Supply Chain Restructuring**  
*Impact Level: Low | Risk Score: 2/5*

Geopolitical tensions and US tariffs on China are restructuring global supply chains. TSMC has adopted a "China for China" strategy, localizing supply chains within China. However, the multilateral trade agreements the US has with Japan and the Netherlands do not include Taiwan, leaving TSMC exposed to asymmetric regulatory burdens.

> *Filing Reference:* [The Diplomat, July 2025](https://thediplomat.com/2025/07/silicon-statecraft-alignment-taiwans-strategic-bet-on-us-led-export-controls/)

### 6.4 Other Regulatory & Compliance Risks

**Patent Litigation Risk in the US**  
*Impact Level: Low | Risk Score: 2/5*

In February 2025, Longitude Licensing Ltd. and Marlin Semiconductor Limited filed complaints with the US ITC and US District Court alleging TSMC and its customers infringe five US patents. The ITC instituted an investigation on March 21, 2025, creating potential disruption to TSMC's US operations and customer relationships.

> *Filing Reference:* [TSMC 2025 Q4 Consolidated Financial Statements](https://investor.tsmc.com/sites/ir/financial-report/2025/TSMC%202025Q4%20Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements_E.pdf)

**Japan & Netherlands Export Restrictions on Semiconductor Equipment**  
*Impact Level: Low | Risk Score: 2/5*

Japan listed 23 types of semiconductor technology subject to export restrictions beginning July 23, 2023, including advanced chip manufacturing equipment. The Netherlands has restricted sales of ASML's EUV tools to China since 2019 and tightened DUV restrictions in March 2023. These multilateral controls affect TSMC's supply chain and the availability of critical manufacturing equipment.
