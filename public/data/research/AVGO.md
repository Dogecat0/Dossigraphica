## 1. Geographic Profile Summary

Broadcom Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in **Palo Alto, California**, and operates as a global technology leader in semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions. The company is organized into two primary segments: **Semiconductor Solutions** (networking, wireless, storage, broadband, and industrial chips) and **Infrastructure Software** (mainframe, cybersecurity, enterprise software, and VMware cloud solutions).

### Global Revenue Distribution (Fiscal Year 2025)

Broadcom's total net revenue reached **$63.9 billion**, with contributions from three major geographic regions:

| Region | Revenue (USD) |
|--------|--------------|
| Americas | $18.9 billion |
| Asia Pacific | $35.9 billion |
| Europe, Middle East & Africa (EMEA) | $9.1 billion |

The **Asia Pacific region** is the largest revenue contributor, accounting for over 56% of total revenue, with significant contributions from **China, Singapore, and Taiwan**. The Americas contributed approximately 30%, while EMEA accounted for roughly 14%.

### Workforce & Operational Footprint

- **Employees:** Over 33,000 worldwide
- **R&D and Manufacturing Facilities:** Spread across **North America, Asia, and Europe**
- **Customer Reach:** Serves customers in **more than 170 countries**
- **Stock Listing:** Trades on the **Nasdaq Global Select Market** under the ticker symbol **AVGO**

Broadcom's extensive geographic footprint underscores its position as a truly global enterprise, with deep penetration in key technology markets across Asia, the Americas, and Europe.

## 3. Revenue Geography

Broadcom's Q1 FY2026 (period ended February 1, 2026) net revenue of **$19,311M** was distributed across three geographic regions. The table below summarizes the regional breakdown, share of total revenue, and year-over-year growth compared to Q1 FY2025.

| Region | Q1 FY2026 Revenue ($M) | % of Total | YoY Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $5,081 | 26.3% | +9.7% |
| Asia Pacific | $11,615 | 60.2% | +44.6% |
| EMEA | $2,615 | 13.5% | +16.1% |
| **Total** | **$19,311** | **100.0%** | **+29.5%** |

### Regional Performance

**Americas ($5,081M, 26.3% of total)** — Revenue grew 9.7% year-over-year from $4,632M in Q1 FY2025. While the Americas posted solid single-digit growth, its share of total revenue declined relative to the outsized expansion in Asia Pacific.

**Asia Pacific ($11,615M, 60.2% of total)** — This region remains Broadcom's largest and fastest-growing market, with revenue surging 44.6% year-over-year from $8,032M. The region's dominant share reflects Broadcom's deep integration into Asian semiconductor supply chains and manufacturing hubs. Key country-level exposures from FY2025 include China (including Hong Kong) at $11,155M, Singapore at $10,796M, and Taiwan at $6,451M.

**EMEA ($2,615M, 13.5% of total)** — Revenue grew 16.1% year-over-year from $2,252M, representing a moderate but steady contribution to the overall geographic mix.

### Geographic Concentration Risk

Asia Pacific accounts for approximately **60% of total revenue**, with significant concentration in China (including Hong Kong), Singapore, and Taiwan. This heavy reliance on a single region — and specifically on countries deeply embedded in global semiconductor supply chains — introduces material geographic concentration risk. Any disruption to trade flows, geopolitical tensions, regulatory changes, or supply chain realignments in the Asia Pacific region could have a disproportionate impact on Broadcom's overall revenue. Investors should monitor developments in U.S.-China trade relations, semiconductor export controls, and regional stability as key risk factors.

- **Penang Logistics Hub:** The Broadcom Primary Warehouse in Penang is the sole title transfer point, creating a bottleneck in the outbound logistics chain.
- **Limited Geographic Diversification:** While the United States, Singapore, and Israel provide some alternative capacity, none can replace TSMC's volume or technology node capability in the near term.

### Apple Inc. — The Anchor Customer

Apple has historically been Broadcom's largest named end-customer, contributing approximately 20% of net revenue in FY2021 and FY2019. Broadcom supplies Apple with a broad portfolio of components including RF front-end modules, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth combo chips, GPS/GNSS system-on-chips (SoCs), and custom touch controllers used across iPhones and other Apple devices. The relationship is deeply embedded in Apple's supply chain, and any disruption — whether from design loss, pricing pressure, or supply chain reconfiguration — would have a material adverse effect on Broadcom's semiconductor revenue.

### The Unnamed Distributor — A Growing Concentration Risk

A single unnamed distributor customer accounted for **32% of net revenue in FY2025** and **42% in Q1 FY2026**, representing a sharp escalation in concentration. This distributor likely serves a broad base of downstream customers, but the concentration at the distributor level means Broadcom's revenue is increasingly dependent on the financial health, inventory management, and ordering patterns of one counterparty. The jump from 32% to 42% in a single quarter warrants close monitoring.

## 6. Regulatory Risk

Broadcom operates across a complex global regulatory landscape spanning trade controls, antitrust enforcement, tax policy, and geopolitical exposure. The following analysis categorizes the most material regulatory risks identified across the company's filings and public disclosures.

### 6.1 Trade & Export Control Risks

**US-China Trade Tensions (Critical)** — Broadcom derives approximately 30% of its revenue from China. Escalation of US-China trade tensions, including proposed 60% tariffs on Chinese goods under the Trump administration, export restrictions, and potential economic decoupling, directly threatens Broadcom's ability to sell products in China and disrupts its supply chain. *(Risk Score: 5/5)*

**Huawei & Entity List Restrictions (High)** — Huawei, a Broadcom customer, is subject to US export restrictions requiring Broadcom to suspend sales until licenses are obtained from the US Department of Commerce. The US government continues to add companies to restricted entity lists, adversely affecting Broadcom's revenue. *(Risk Score: 4/5)*

**CFIUS & National Security Review (Moderate)** — Broadcom's proposed $117 billion hostile takeover of Qualcomm was blocked by the Trump administration in March 2018 via executive order citing national security concerns. Future acquisitions face heightened CFIUS scrutiny and potential blockage. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

**US Export Controls & Restricted Entity List Expansion (Moderate)** — Broadcom is subject to US Export Administration Regulations. The ongoing expansion of restricted entity lists and prohibited export technologies has had and will continue to have an adverse effect on Broadcom's ability to sell products globally. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

### 6.2 Antitrust & Competition Investigations

**EU Antitrust Investigations & VMware Licensing Complaint (Moderate)** — Broadcom has been involved in regulatory investigations in the European Union. In March 2026, CISPE filed a formal complaint alleging Broadcom's VMware licensing infringed EU antitrust rules. Broadcom previously committed to suspend exclusivity agreements for seven years following an EU interim order. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

**Korean Fair Trade Commission Investigation (Moderate)** — Broadcom is subject to an ongoing investigation by the Korean Fair Trade Commission into certain of its contracting and business practices. Such investigations can be costly, lengthy, and divert management attention. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

**FTC Antitrust Investigation & Settlement (Moderate)** — In 2021, Broadcom agreed to settle an FTC antitrust complaint alleging abuse of monopoly power through restrictive contract terms. The FTC continues to investigate Broadcom's contracting practices, posing ongoing regulatory compliance risk. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

### 6.3 Tax Policy & Incentive Expiration Risks

**Singapore Tax Incentive Expiration & Global Minimum Tax (High)** — Broadcom's principal tax incentives from the Singapore Economic Development Board are scheduled to expire through November 2030. The corporate income tax rate in Singapore would otherwise be 17%. The enactment of the global minimum tax (Pillar Two) in Singapore effective FY2026 will materially impact consolidated results and cash flows. *(Risk Score: 4/5)*

**Malaysia Tax Holiday Expiration (High)** — Broadcom has a tax holiday on qualifying income in Malaysia scheduled to expire in fiscal year 2028. Non-compliance with operating conditions could require refund of previously realized material tax benefits. The tax holiday decreased the provision for income taxes by approximately $2,709 million for FY2025. *(Risk Score: 4/5)*

**One Big Beautiful Bill Act & Valuation Allowance (High)** — On July 4, 2025, the US enacted the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. Broadcom established a $1,321 million valuation allowance against its federal corporate alternative minimum tax credit carryforwards, materially impacting financial results. *(Risk Score: 4/5)*

### 6.4 Geopolitical & Operational Concentration Risks

**TSMC Dependency & Taiwan Strait Tensions (Critical)** — Broadcom depends on TSMC (Taiwan) for a meaningful portion of its total production capacity. Geopolitical turmoil involving China-Taiwan relations could disrupt wafer supply, severely impacting Broadcom's semiconductor revenue. *(Risk Score: 5/5)*

**Sole-Source Facility Risk: Fort Collins FBAR Filters (High)** — The Fort Collins, Colorado facility is the sole source for FBAR filters used in many of Broadcom's wireless devices. Any disruption at this facility would severely impact Broadcom's ability to manufacture FBAR products and adversely affect its wireless business. *(Risk Score: 4/5)*

**Sole-Source Facility Risk: Breinigsville InP Wafers (High)** — The Breinigsville, Pennsylvania facility is the sole source for InP-based wafers used in Broadcom's fiber optics products. Any disruption would severely impact Broadcom's ability to manufacture fiber optics products. *(Risk Score: 4/5)*

**Facility Concentration in Seismic/Weather-Prone Pacific Rim (Moderate)** — Many of Broadcom's facilities and those of its contract manufacturers are located in California and the Pacific Rim region, which have above-average seismic activity and severe weather activity. Disruptions could limit capacity to meet customer demands. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

**Foreign Exchange & Commodity Price Exposure (Moderate)** — Broadcom enters into foreign exchange forward contracts to hedge exposures, but these strategies may not be effective. Broadcom does not hedge its exposure to commodity prices (including gold and fuel), which are very volatile and could adversely affect gross margin. *(Risk Score: 3/5)*

---

**Summary:** Broadcom faces two critical regulatory risks (US-China trade tensions and TSMC/Taiwan dependency), six high-level risks spanning export controls, tax policy changes, and sole-source facility concentration, and six moderate risks across antitrust investigations, CFIUS scrutiny, and operational exposures. The concentration of critical and high risks in the Asia-Pacific region and US underscores the importance of geographic diversification and proactive regulatory engagement.
