## 5. Customer Concentration

As of Q1 2026, AMD maintains a highly diversified and strategic customer base, characterized by deep integration with global hyperscalers and emerging AI infrastructure leaders. The company's revenue dependencies are increasingly tied to long-term, high-volume deployments of its Instinct GPU and EPYC CPU product lines.

### 5.1 Hyperscale Cloud Partnerships
AMD continues to solidify its position as a primary silicon provider for the world's largest cloud service providers. These partnerships are centered on the expansion of 5th Gen EPYC-powered cloud instances, ensuring a steady revenue stream from:

* **AWS (Seattle, USA):** Expanding EPYC-powered cloud infrastructure.
* **Google Cloud (Mountain View, USA):** Scaling EPYC-based compute instances.
* **Microsoft Azure (Redmond, USA):** Ongoing integration of 5th Gen EPYC processors.
* **Tencent (Shenzhen, China):** Continued deployment of EPYC-powered cloud services.

### 5.2 Strategic AI Infrastructure & GPU Deployments
Beyond general-purpose cloud compute, AMD has secured critical, high-value contracts focused on large-scale AI training and inference infrastructure:

* **Meta (Menlo Park, USA):** A landmark multiyear deal to deploy up to 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs, positioning AMD as a cornerstone of Meta's AI data center strategy.
* **OpenAI (San Francisco, USA):** Secured as a key customer for the new Helios rack-scale system, signaling strong demand for AMD's integrated AI hardware solutions.
* **Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) (Mumbai, India):** A strategic collaboration to co-develop Helios-based rack-scale AI infrastructure, expanding AMD's footprint in the Indian market.
* **NAVER Cloud (Seongnam, South Korea):** A collaborative effort to deploy a comprehensive stack of AMD Instinct GPUs and EPYC CPUs to bolster AI infrastructure in South Korea.

### 5.3 Geographic Distribution
AMD's customer concentration reflects a global footprint, with significant operations in North America, Asia-Pacific, and India. This geographic spread mitigates regional market volatility while allowing the company to capitalize on localized AI infrastructure build-outs. While specific revenue shares remain proprietary, the shift toward multiyear, large-scale infrastructure deals suggests a transition toward more predictable, long-term revenue cycles.
